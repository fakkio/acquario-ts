# Prior art: how evolved-organism simulators cost body parts

Research for issue #43 (child of the v0.2 map #39). It feeds the organelle cost law, which ADR-0014 leaves open. Question: how do evolved-organism simulators cost body parts, and has any of them produced stable **specialists** (producers vs consumers) rather than generalists? By what mechanism?

Every claim cites the source that owns it: the simulator's source code or its official docs, or the paper itself. Code is cited at a pinned commit where one exists. Where a statement is my own inference or arithmetic rather than a source's, it is marked **(inference)**.

## Answer in brief

1. **No surveyed simulator uses sublinear effectiveness in part size.** Every one uses a **linear** cost law: a flat cost per part, or a cost proportional to size, area or activity. Diminishing returns show up only indirectly, through geometry or through internal area being zero-sum (§1).
2. **Where specialists exist, the simulator's own rules put them there.** None of them got specialists out of a cost curve. There are three ways they do it:
   - **Legislated incompatibility.** The Life Engine switches producer cells off on any organism that can move. Polyworld and Gene Pool make diet a discrete gene.
   - **A single trade-off axis with a negative floor.** The Bibites' diet gene and evolv.io's mouth hue work this way: the wrong food costs you energy.
   - **A fixed per-part budget.** Framsticks, before 2023, gave each part one budget that assimilation, ingestion, stamina and muscle had to share.
3. **Theory agrees with ADR-0014.** Rueffler, Hermisson & Wagner (2012) show that specialisation is favoured by _accelerating_ performance functions, positional effects and synergy, and that robustness pushes the other way. Sublinear (decelerating) effectiveness therefore favours the generalist. This is a known result, not a flaw in AcquarioTS's model.
4. **Two known mechanisms rescue specialisation without superlinearity:**
   - **A flat per-capability overhead** (ADR-0014's option 2). Under sublinear effectiveness it favours specialists **only below a size threshold** that grows with investment (inference, §4).
   - **Frequency-dependent competition for distinct depletable resources.** In Avida this produced adaptive radiation _in a spatially homogeneous environment_ (Chow et al. 2004). That weakens ADR-0014's claim that the ecological route needs spatial fluid fields.
5. **Plankton models state ADR-0014's exact question as one exponent.** Ward & Follows (2016) scale a mixotroph's autotrophic and heterotrophic rates by `ω^τ` and `(1−ω)^τ`. With `τ < 1` the generalist is favoured, and with `τ > 1` specialists are. Real mixotrophs persist despite paying extra metabolic costs, because under diffusion limitation they can take up both resource forms (Ward et al. 2011).

## 1. How each simulator costs body parts

| Simulator             | What is costed                                                                                                         | Shape of the law                                                                                                                                            | Effectiveness vs size                                                              |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Polyworld**         | Every behaviour activation, neurons, synapses, a fixed drain                                                           | Linear in activation. Brain cost is linear in neuron and synapse counts. Movement cost is linear in size. The whole sum is multiplied by the Strength gene. | Linear: size raises movement cost, max energy and fight advantage, all linearly    |
| **Framsticks**        | Idle metabolism per stick. Muscle static and dynamic work.                                                             | Flat per part ("per stick") plus activity                                                                                                                   | A per-part budget split four ways (until 2023)                                     |
| **The Bibites** (0.6) | Base metabolism ∝ size (2D) × metabolism speed. Neuron and synapse upkeep and birth costs. Growth cost per body point. | Area-linear. Metabolic efficiency = `efficiency^metabolismSpeed`, so speed costs superlinearly.                                                             | Organs share the internal area through normalised "WAG" weights, which is zero-sum |
| **evolv.io**          | Metabolism ∝ energy (body area). Turning and fighting ∝ energy. Accelerating, eating and swimming.                     | Area-linear plus activity                                                                                                                                   | No part-level organs                                                               |
| **Gene Pool**         | A continual drain plus a cost per unit of stroke amplitude                                                             | Flat plus activity                                                                                                                                          | No explicit per-part upkeep                                                        |
| **The Life Engine**   | Food needed to reproduce = cell count. Lifespan = cells × 100.                                                         | Flat per cell                                                                                                                                               | Linear per cell                                                                    |
| **Avida**             | Implicitly, CPU cycles. Base merit ∝ genome size, compensating the longer copy time.                                   | Per instruction                                                                                                                                             | Rewards per task are multiplicative (`pow`)                                        |

Details and sources:

- **Polyworld.** Per tick, energy used is `eat·eat2Energy + mate·mate2Energy + fight·fight2Energy + speed·speed2Energy + |dyaw|·yaw2Energy + brainEnergy + fixedEnergyDrain`. The sum is then multiplied by `Strength() · (1 + ageEnergyMultiplier·age)` ([agent.cc L1178–1202](https://github.com/polyworld/polyworld/blob/99debe8c40fd9f8e58eaae49bb86a68fd1af3703/src/library/agent/agent.cc#L1178-L1202)). Brain energy is `maxneuron2energy·numNeurons/maxneurons + maxsynapse2energy·numSynapses/maxsynapses` ([GroupsBrain.cc L616–617](https://github.com/polyworld/polyworld/blob/99debe8c40fd9f8e58eaae49bb86a68fd1af3703/src/library/brain/groups/GroupsBrain.cc#L616-L617)). Size scales the movement and turning cost linearly, between `MinSizeEnergyPenalty` and `MaxSizeEnergyPenalty` (default 10), and gives a linear `SizeAdvantage` in fights ([agent.cc L729–751](https://github.com/polyworld/polyworld/blob/99debe8c40fd9f8e58eaae49bb86a68fd1af3703/src/library/agent/agent.cc#L729-L751); defaults in [worldfile.wfs](https://github.com/polyworld/polyworld/blob/99debe8c40fd9f8e58eaae49bb86a68fd1af3703/etc/worldfile.wfs)). Yaeger's own summary: "Organisms expend energy with each action, including neural activity; more actions, more neurons, or more synapses produce greater energy expenditures" ([Yaeger 1993, Ars Electronica](https://webarchive.ars.electronica.art/en/archiv_files/19931/E1993_122.pdf)).
- **Framsticks.** "Idle metabolism: energy requirement for one living stick per one simulation step." "Muscle static work, Muscle dynamic work, Assimilation productivity: these settings influence energy balance" ([standard expdef reference](https://www.framsticks.com/a/doc_expdef-standard.html), [simulation parameters](https://www.framsticks.com/a/al_params.html)). Muscle strength: "strong muscles act with bigger force, gain higher speed, can resist bigger stress, and use more energy" ([Framsticks Manual](https://www.framsticks.com/files/common/Framsticks_Manual.pdf), §genotype f1).
- **The Bibites.** Metabolism cost = `Default Metabolism Cost × Metabolism Speed × Size(2D)` (wiki marks this page as pre-0.6: [Metabolism](https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Metabolism)). Metabolism Speed is split into a rate and an efficiency, with efficiency = `energyEfficiencySetting^metabolismSpeed`, and "affects every single energy use", brain upkeep included ([Metabolism Speed](https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Metabolism_Speed)). The cost per body point rises with the vision and strength genes ([Body](https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Body)). In 0.6.0, "7 internal organs … take up actual physical space inside them and compete for it. The WAGG genes … control how much space inside the bibite is taken up by each organ" ([devlog 0.6.0](https://thebibites.itch.io/the-bibites/devlog/809631/the-bibites-060-organs-and-science)). "Like all other WAG genes, the size of the stomach scales proportionally to the size of the bibite" ([Stomach WAG](https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Stomach_WAG)). Neurons and synapses have separate birth and upkeep costs ([Neuron Birth Cost](https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Neuron_Birth_Cost)).
- **evolv.io.** `metabolize: loseEnergy(energy*METABOLISM_ENERGY*timeStep)`. Radius is `sqrt(energy/ENERGY_DENSITY/π)`, so this cost is proportional to area. Turning and fighting are also multiplied by `energy` ([Creature.pde L2–11, L291–346](https://github.com/awwong1/evolve.io/blob/11cbd050e61774d0042af16bd80c21bbbc48032d/Creature.pde#L291-L346), a mirror of carykh's source).
- **Gene Pool.** `_energy -= CONTINUAL_ENERGY_DRAIN` every tick, and `_energy -= |strokeAmplitude| * ENERGY_USED_UP_SWIMMING` per part stroke ([Swimbot.js](https://www.swimbots.com/genepool/simulation/Swimbot.js), Ventrella's served source). "Swimbots expend energy by moving their parts" ([Ventrella, _GenePool_](https://www.ventrella.com/Alife/GenePool.pdf), §1.2.7).
- **The Life Engine.** `foodNeeded() = cells.length (+ extraMoverFoodCost if mover)`, `lifespan() = cells.length * lifespanMultiplier` ([Organism.js L47–53](https://github.com/MaxRobinsonTheGreat/LifeEngine/blob/15bb2fe444d3c5c0024dc92183fbda8249813fbb/src/Organism/Organism.js#L47-L53)).
- **Avida.** The default `BASE_MERIT_METHOD 4` makes merit proportional to min(executed, copied) genome size ([avida.cfg](https://github.com/devosoft/avida/blob/master/avida-core/support/config/avida.cfg)). Tasks are rewarded `type=pow`, meaning "Multiply the current merit by 2^bonus", with values 1 to 5 in the [default environment.cfg](https://github.com/devosoft/avida/blob/master/avida-core/support/config/environment.cfg) ([Environment file docs](https://github.com/devosoft/avida/wiki/Environment-file)).

**Pattern (inference).** Every simulator surveyed costs parts linearly: flat per part, proportional to area, or proportional to activity. None of them makes the _benefit_ of a single part sublinear in its size by rule. Where diminishing returns exist, they come from geometry (evolv.io's area-scaled metabolism against contact-based eating) or from a zero-sum share of internal space (Bibites WAG, Framsticks' four-way budget). AcquarioTS's "flat overhead + area-scaled cost + sublinear effectiveness" has no direct precedent here. Its closest relatives are the Bibites (area-proportional organs sharing internal space) and Framsticks (flat per-part upkeep).

## 2. How specialists arise, per simulator

- **The Life Engine: legislated.** `ProducerCell.performFunction` returns early `if (this.org.anatomy.is_mover && !Hyperparams.moversCanProduce)`, and `moversCanProduce = false` by default ([ProducerCell.js](https://github.com/MaxRobinsonTheGreat/LifeEngine/blob/15bb2fe444d3c5c0024dc92183fbda8249813fbb/src/Organism/Cell/BodyCells/ProducerCell.js), [Hyperparameters.js](https://github.com/MaxRobinsonTheGreat/LifeEngine/blob/15bb2fe444d3c5c0024dc92183fbda8249813fbb/src/Hyperparameters.js)). The UI tooltip reads: "When off, producer cells are disabled on mover organisms." The producer/consumer split is written as a rule. This is the "species type declares the answer" that ADR-0014 rejects.
- **Polyworld: discrete metabolisms.** `AgentMetabolisms` is a list of named metabolisms, each with an `EnergyPolarity` per food energy type in {−1, 0, 1}. Selection is by `Gene` or `Random` ([Metabolism.h](https://github.com/polyworld/polyworld/blob/99debe8c40fd9f8e58eaae49bb86a68fd1af3703/src/library/agent/Metabolism.h), [worldfile.wfs `AgentMetabolisms`](https://github.com/polyworld/polyworld/blob/99debe8c40fd9f8e58eaae49bb86a68fd1af3703/etc/worldfile.wfs)). No generalist phenotype can exist. The "species" Yaeger reports ("frenetic joggers", "indolent cannibals", "edge runners") are _behavioural_ strategies with a single metabolism, not trophic specialists ([Yaeger 1993](https://webarchive.ars.electronica.art/en/archiv_files/19931/E1993_122.pdf)).
- **Gene Pool: discrete digestibility gene plus a penalty.** With `numFoodTypes === 2`, `digestibleFoodType = floor(gene*2)`, and eating the other type multiplies energy by `FOOD_TYPE_OFFSET = 0.2` ([Embryology.js](https://www.swimbots.com/genepool/simulation/Embryology.js), [Parameters.js](https://www.swimbots.com/genepool/simulation/Parameters.js), [Swimbot.js](https://www.swimbots.com/genepool/simulation/Swimbot.js)). Research notes left in `Parameters.js` describe "food niche" runs with offsets of 0.1 and then 1.0 as a control ("so the food type gene has no effect"). The niche mechanism was being tested, not assumed.
- **The Bibites: one linear axis with a negative floor.** Affinity to meat equals the diet gene, and affinity to plants equals `1 − diet`. Conversion efficiency is `E = Emin + (Emax − Emin)·affinity`, with default plant range −50%…50% and meat range −30%…85%. A full carnivore therefore digests plants at −50% efficiency and loses energy eating them. The same page notes: "It is however planned to … allow parallel affinities to exist at a cost." ([Bibite Stomach](https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Bibite_Stomach); older form on [Diet](https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Diet)). **(inference)** With income linear in `diet` and one food dominant locally, the optimum is a corner. Specialisation here follows from a _linear_ trade-off plus negative efficiencies, and the "parallel affinities at a cost" plan is a per-capability overhead.
- **evolv.io: tent-shaped efficiency on a brain output.** `multiplier = 1 − |tile.foodType − mouthHue| / FOOD_SENSITIVITY` with `FOOD_SENSITIVITY = 0.3`. A negative multiplier drains energy. Tiles carry a climate-derived food hue ([Creature.pde L320–340](https://github.com/awwong1/evolve.io/blob/11cbd050e61774d0042af16bd80c21bbbc48032d/Creature.pde#L320-L340), [Tile.pde](https://github.com/awwong1/evolve.io/blob/11cbd050e61774d0042af16bd80c21bbbc48032d/Tile.pde)). `mouthHue` is a _neural output_, not a gene, so this is behavioural plasticity paired with a spatially heterogeneous environment.
- **Framsticks: a per-part budget, since removed.** `normalizeBiol4()` forced `muscle_power + assimilation + stamina + ingestion = 1` on every part: a linear per-module trade-off. Its removal is commented: "before 2023-05, the four fields below were aggregated and normalized using normalizeBiol4() … If at all, this should be handled by the expdef depending on the goal of the experiment (e.g., to encourage specialization)" ([geneprops.cpp](https://www.framsticks.com/svn/framsticks/cpp/frams/genetics/geneprops.cpp), accessed 2026-09-27). The manual also gives a _positional_ effect: "a vertical stick can assimilate twice as much as a horizontal one" ([Manual](https://www.framsticks.com/files/common/Framsticks_Manual.pdf)).
- **Avida: ecology, not cost.** Under the default unlimited-resource `pow` rewards, merit multiplies across tasks. That is a synergistic combination, which favours doing everything. Specialisation comes from the environment instead:
  - With depletable resources in fixed-size populations, "maximum species richness emerges at intermediate productivity, even in a spatially homogeneous environment, owing to frequency-dependent selection to exploit an influx of mixed resources. A diverse pool of limiting resources is sufficient to cause adaptive radiation" ([Chow et al. 2004, _Science_ 305:84](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1096307)).
  - In a narrow environment where only one task pays, generalists lost unused functions, "most often from the accumulation of neutral and deleterious mutations", and beneficial mutations "were disproportionately likely to cause losses of function" ([Ostrowski, Ofria & Lenski 2007, _Am Nat_ 169:E1](https://doi.org/10.1086/510211)).

## 3. What theory says about the cost-curve shape

- **Levins' fitness sets (1962).** When the set of attainable (performance-on-A, performance-on-B) pairs is convex, the generalist is optimal. When it is concave, a specialist is ([Levins 1962, _Am Nat_ 96:361](https://doi.org/10.1086/282245), the classical result that Egas et al. and Rueffler et al. build on).
- **Rueffler, Hermisson & Wagner (2012).** This is the model closest to "organelles as modules". Modules contribute to two tasks with a trade-off. "Division of labor is favored by three factors: positional effects that predispose modules for one of the tasks, accelerating performance functions, and synergistic interactions between modules. If modules can be lost or damaged, selection for robustness can counteract selection for functional specialization" ([PNAS 109:E326](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1110521109), [PMC3277576](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3277576/)). The robustness effect is strongest when fitness is multiplicative in the two tasks, i.e. when both are essential.
- **Egas, Dieckmann & Sabelis (2004).** Specialist–generalist coexistence "is much more restricted than originally thought and strongly depends on the trade-off structure". It is "less likely for species facing a trade-off between per capita reproduction in different habitats than when the trade-off acts on carrying capacities alone". Even when ecologically stable, such coexistence "cannot be reached through gradual evolution" in most cases, only "through immigration from elsewhere or through mutations of large effect" ([_Am Nat_ 163:518](https://doi.org/10.1086/382599)).
- **Mixotroph plankton models state the trade-off as one exponent.** Ward & Follows (2016): "the autotrophic parameters … are multiplied by ω^τ, whereas the heterotrophic parameters are multiplied by (1−ω)^τ". With ω = 0.5 and τ ∈ {0.74, 1.00, 1.32}, a mixotroph gets ≈60%, 50% or 40% of the specialist rates. In their main runs, "mixotrophy was incorporated without consideration of any potential costs", and the authors call equal rates "intuitively unlikely" ([PNAS 113:2958](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1517118113), [PMC4801304](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4801304/)). Ward et al. (2011) note that mixotrophy's flexibility "must be traded off against an associated increase in metabolic costs, which appear to make mixotrophs uncompetitive relative to obligate autotrophs and heterotrophs". Their model still finds a regime where mixotrophs win: under diffusion-limited uptake at low concentrations, they can take in both resource forms, which "can be enough to offset as much as a twofold increase in additional metabolic costs" ([_Am Nat_ 178:98](https://doi.org/10.1086/660284)).

## 4. What this means for the AcquarioTS cost law

These points are inference. They are for the organelle-cost-law grilling ticket to weigh, not decisions.

**Sublinear effectiveness is the plankton models' `τ < 1`.** ADR-0014's contradiction is the known result: decelerating returns favour the generalist (Rueffler; Ward & Follows).

**A flat per-capability overhead gives a size threshold, not a blanket incentive.** Assume two substitutable income routes with effectiveness `k·a^τ`, a total organelle area `A`, and a per-capability overhead `c`. The area-scaled cost is linear in total area, so it cancels at fixed `A`. Then:

```text
generalist (even split):  2·k·(A/2)^τ − 2c  =  2^(1−τ)·k·A^τ − 2c
specialist:               k·A^τ − c
specialist wins  ⇔  c > (2^(1−τ) − 1)·k·A^τ        e.g. τ = 0.5 → c > 0.41·k·√A
```

So small investors specialise and large ones generalise. At `τ = 1` any `c > 0` favours the specialist.

**Positional effects are already in AcquarioTS's model.** `vision.md` says "position matters", and photosynthesis scales with projected width. With v0.2 rotation, gravity and buoyancy, the side facing the light could predispose a chloroplast the way Framsticks' vertical sticks assimilate twice as much. That is Rueffler's first factor, and it costs no new law.

**The ecological route may not need fluid fields.** Chow et al. obtained radiation in a _spatially homogeneous_ world from distinct depletable resources plus frequency dependence. AcquarioTS's closed carbon ledger (ADR-0001) already makes its pools depletable. Whether producers and consumers are limited by _different_ pools (light and CO₂ against food and O₂) decides whether this applies before v0.4.

**The axis framing is worth checking.** The literature's trade-off applies to _substitutable_ routes to one resource, such as fixing food against taking it in. In `vision.md`, respiration is the energy step _every_ organism needs, so it combines _multiplicatively_ with either food route. Rueffler predicts robustness and generalism on such an axis. ADR-0014 names the axis "photosynthesis/respiration". The divergence the literature supports is chloroplast against food intake.

**Specialists will be hard to reach by small steps.** Egas et al. say gradual evolution rarely reaches specialist–generalist coexistence, and large-effect mutations help. An organelle insertion is such a mutation. The v0.2 constraint that organelles "tend small" when they arise works against a large effect.

**What each simulator's "specialists" would mean for AcquarioTS.** Every example here is either legislated (Life Engine, Polyworld, Gene Pool), a single linear axis with negative floors (Bibites, evolv.io), or ecological (Avida). None comes from a sublinear cost curve. Under ADR-0014's own rule ("a law of the world, not a designed outcome"):

- The Life Engine's rule is out.
- A Bibites-style linear axis is a law, but it hard-codes the trade-off.
- The overhead and ecological routes are the ones consistent with the project's bet.
